Gate 1b: receiver dependence

The same transverse history direction is much more unusual to the value head than to the policy head. At |λ|=2, real value-logit RMS sensitivity is about 1.88× the mean matched-control sensitivity, association change is 1.32×, and policy-logit RMS is only 1.12×.

state present ≠ state equally readable by every downstream head

This is an artificial-network readout result, not evidence for a biological spike code.

Gate 2: make “ping as query” literal

For each position, the bank stores the penultimate settling state and its orthogonal history coordinate. The final state-bearing ping tries to retrieve its own predecessor. Present and history coordinates use fixed cosine similarity; the history arm averages them equally.

Falsifier fired

The real history coordinate did not retrieve its own recorded settling predecessor better. It made retrieval worse than both present-only and shuffled-history controls, and the mean correct margin became negative. This rejects the literal Gate 2 coordinate/metric on this smoke set.

What changed conceptually

Gate 1b and Gate 2 now separate two ideas that were easy to blur together. A history-bearing direction can be readable by trained downstream geometry without being a good explicit content-addressable key under an imposed cosine metric. The first survived its smoke test; the second did not.

history readable by a learned receiver ≠ history is a retrieval key

That leaves a narrower next question: if a ping acts query-like in a biological or learned system, the relevant reader/geometry may itself have to be learned for retrieval. This experiment does not test transformer attention, biological episodic memory, or consciousness.

Current reading

The useful result tonight is the split. Gate 1b says the tiny orthogonal temporal component is not generic to every frozen reader. Gate 2 says we cannot jump from that to “the ping searches recorded histories” using this explicit memory bank and equal-weight cosine score. The stronger metaphor was tested and lost.
